## Findings

**No vulnerabilities in this review met the skill's reporting threshold of confidence 0.7 or higher.**

The following patterns were examined and verified secure:
- **Reward calculation & fund safety:** In `src/Oracle.sol`, `submitValue()` computes the submitter reward using `calculateReward(rewardPool, w, timeSinceUser, newQ)` with `newQ` in the denominator. Because $w \le \text{newQ}$ and the activity factor is bounded in $[0, 1]$, the reward fraction $\frac{w \cdot \text{activityFactor}}{\text{newQ}} \le 1$, guaranteeing that `rewardToSubmitter <= rewardPool <= address(this).balance` and preventing balance exhaustion reverts.
- **Reentrancy:** State-modifying entry points (`submitValue`, `depositTokens`, `withdrawTokens`) utilize OpenZeppelin's `ReentrancyGuard` (`nonReentrant` modifier) and adhere strictly to the Checks-Effects-Interactions (CEI) pattern.
- **Fixed-point arithmetic & division safety:** All prices are standardized to 18 decimals (`WAD = 1e18`). `ComposedOracle.sol` verifies non-zero denominators (`priceB == 0` check reverting with custom error `DivisionByZero()`) before division and inversion operations.
- **Access control & permissioning:** Core administrative configurations use OpenZeppelin's `Ownable`. Token weight validation and voter whitelisting enforce deposit locking periods before voting or submission weights become active.
- **Loop gas limits:** All iteration over price history (`readValueInterval`, `getPriceHistoryRange`) is bounded by `sampleSize` or index ranges, preventing denial-of-service through block gas limits.

## Notes

### Note 1: Historical Lookback Clamping in `readValueInterval` — `src/Oracle.sol:220` and `src/ComposedOracle.sol:116`

- Category: design-tradeoff
- Observation: When consumer protocols invoke `readValueInterval()`, if the requested `sampleSize` exceeds the available historical sample length (`sampleSize > historyLength`), the contract clamps `sampleSize` to `historyLength` rather than reverting.
- Rationale: This is a documented, intentional protocol design choice ensuring protocol liveness and preventing consumer contract reverts during initial oracle bootstrapping when fewer samples than the target lookback period have been recorded.
